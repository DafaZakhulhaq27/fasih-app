Matching headings: find the main idea

Paragraph B of the passage you just read. Look at where the main idea sits.

B

When the first tablets reached classrooms, many schools bought them in large numbers. Yet a decade later, researchers found that the devices themselves made little difference to results. What mattered was how teachers used them: the same tablet could support careful practice in one room and simple distraction in another.

main idea, right after “Yet”

Two habits for this question type

Read the first two sentences and the last one

The main idea often comes after a turn: yet, however, but, in fact.

Match the meaning, not the words

A heading that repeats a word from the paragraph, like “tablets”, is often the trap.

A good heading here: “The tool matters less than the teaching”.

A heading sums up the whole paragraph, not one example.
